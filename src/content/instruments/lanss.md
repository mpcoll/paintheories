## Overview

Bennett developed the LANSS from the sensory descriptions and bedside sensory findings of patients with nociceptive and neuropathic pain, combining them in a seven-item scale with a simple scoring system [@bennett2001]. The two examination items test for allodynia and altered pinprick threshold [@bennett2001]. The S-LANSS replaces the clinician's examination with two self-examination items so that it can be completed unaided, for example in postal surveys [@bennett2005; @torrance2014]; population studies count an S-LANSS score of 12 or more as chronic pain with neuropathic characteristics [@torrance2014].

## Strengths and limits

Positive scores are meant to identify pain of predominantly neuropathic origin, that is, pain dominated by neuropathic mechanisms [@bennett2007]. Independent appraisal of the measurement properties of the English LANSS was less favourable than for the DN4 [@mathieson2015], and, as with all screening tools, a positive score calls for clinical assessment rather than replacing it [@bennett2007].
