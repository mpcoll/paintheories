## Overview

The PAINAD was developed from the literature, related observational tools and expert clinicians as a simple scale for people with advanced dementia who can no longer rate their own pain [@warden2003]. The observer watches the person and scores five behaviours, each from 0 to 2, to give a total from 0 to 10.

## Strengths and limits

It is short and was designed to be easy to use at the bedside. In the development study it was reliable between raters, correlated with an established discomfort scale and fell after as-needed analgesia [@warden2003]. That study was small and included mostly men, and its authors called for further research before universal recommendation [@warden2003]. Like other behavioural scales it records an observer's judgement of behaviour, not the person's own rating.
