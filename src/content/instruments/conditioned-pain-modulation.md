## Overview

Conditioned pain modulation is the human psychophysical counterpart of diffuse noxious inhibitory control: pain at one site is reduced by a second painful stimulus elsewhere [@yarnitsky2010]. A test stimulus is rated alone and again with a conditioning stimulus, and the difference is the CPM effect. Because protocols differ between laboratories, a group of researchers published recommendations on how to run and report the test [@yarnitsky2015].

## In research and practice

As a group, people with chronic pain show less CPM than healthy controls [@lewis2012], including people with fibromyalgia [@obrien2018]. In painful diabetic neuropathy, less efficient CPM predicted a better response to duloxetine, a drug thought to enhance descending inhibition [@yarnitsky2012]. Reliability can be good but depends on the stimuli and methods used [@kennedy2016], and differences in protocol, age, sex and blinding add variability between studies [@lewis2012; @obrien2018]. Its use as a biomarker for diagnosis or treatment choice in individual patients still needs validation [@obrien2018].
