## Overview

Hill and colleagues selected single screening questions for physical and psychosocial prognostic factors that predict persistent disabling back pain, and set cut-offs for three risk groups [@hill2008]. The tool is meant to guide initial decisions in primary care [@hill2008]. In the STarT Back trial, low-risk patients received a single session of assessment, advice and education, medium-risk patients were referred for physiotherapy, and high-risk patients for psychologically informed physiotherapy that also addressed psychosocial obstacles to recovery [@hill2011].

## In research and practice

In the STarT Back trial (851 patients in English general practice), stratified care using the tool with matched treatment pathways improved Roland-Morris disability scores more than current best practice at 4 months (between-group difference 1.81 points) and 12 months (1.06 points), and saved costs; within risk groups, the 12-month difference was significant for medium-risk but not high-risk patients [@hill2011]. The benefit depends on the matched treatments, not on the screening itself: when an adaptation was implemented in US primary care, clinicians used the tool for about half of their patients but did not change their treatment recommendations, and patient outcomes did not improve [@cherkin2018].
