## Overview

The Oswestry Low Back Pain Disability Questionnaire was published by Fairbank and colleagues in 1980 [@fairbank1980]. Twenty years later its developers reviewed the versions in use: four in English and nine in other languages, with some published versions containing misprints and many omitting the scoring system [@fairbank2000]. The developers recommend version 2.1a [@chiarotto2016].

## Strengths and limits

The ODI has become one of the principal condition-specific outcome measures in spinal disorders [@fairbank2000], and IMMPACT lists it with the RMDQ as a well-validated disease-specific measure of physical functioning in low back pain [@dworkin2008]. Compared head to head with the RMDQ, it has better test-retest reliability and smaller measurement error but weaker construct validity as a measure of physical functioning, and the review found no strong reason to prefer either [@chiarotto2016]. Its developers noted that the instrument's behaviour is incompletely understood, particularly its sensitivity to real change [@fairbank2000]. A change of 10 points has been proposed as the minimal important change [@ostelo2008].
