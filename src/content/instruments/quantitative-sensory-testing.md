## Overview

Quantitative sensory testing applies graded, standardised stimuli and records what the person detects and when it becomes painful. The protocol of the German Research Network on Neuropathic Pain (DFNS) covers thermal detection and pain thresholds, paradoxical heat sensations, mechanical detection thresholds (von Frey filaments, 64 Hz tuning fork), pinprick and pressure pain thresholds, stimulus-response functions for pinprick and dynamic mechanical allodynia, and the wind-up ratio [@rolke2006]. Each result is compared with reference values as a z-score, so that a profile of gain (hyperalgesia, allodynia) and loss (hypoesthesia, hypoalgesia) of function can be drawn [@rolke2006].

## In research and practice

With trained examiners the protocol is reliable within and between observers over two days [@geber2011]. Grouping patients by their sensory profiles rather than by diagnosis gave three replicable clusters in peripheral neuropathic pain, which may relate to different mechanisms and treatment responses [@baron2017]. The EFNS guideline found QST useful for indicating probable neuropathic pain and for assessing provoked pains and treatment response [@cruccu2010]. The NeuPSIG guideline recommends it for selected cases in the clinic, including the diagnosis of small fibre neuropathies, and for research [@haanpaa2011]. The full battery takes time and equipment, depends on the person's attention and cooperation, and some losses of function are hard to detect in an individual [@rolke2006].
