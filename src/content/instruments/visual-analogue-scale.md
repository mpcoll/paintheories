## Overview

The visual analogue scale asks the person to mark a point on an unmarked line that best shows how much it hurts. Scoring is the measured distance from the "no pain" end. Anchor wording varies between versions: one review counted 24 different descriptors used to anchor the ends of NRS, VRS and VAS scales [@hjermstad2011].

Verbal rating scales (VRS) are the other classic format: the person picks one of a few ordered words, such as none, mild, moderate or severe. IMMPACT recommends such a categorical rating where numerical ratings may be problematic, and notes that people who cannot complete an NRS may still manage a VRS [@dworkin2005]. Each VRS category covers a wide spread of NRS scores [@hjermstad2011], so the categories are coarse.

## Strengths and limits

The VAS gives a fine-grained, near-continuous score, and VAS and NRS scores correspond overall [@hjermstad2011]. Its weaknesses are practical. VAS measures usually have more missing and incomplete data than NRS measures, difficulty completing them increases with age and opioid intake, and patients tend to prefer VRS and NRS formats; the scales also differ in how easily they can be given by telephone or in electronic diaries [@dworkin2005]. For these reasons IMMPACT chose the 0 to 10 NRS as its core intensity measure [@dworkin2005]. In low back pain, a change of 15 points on the 0 to 100 VAS has been proposed as the minimal important change [@ostelo2008].
