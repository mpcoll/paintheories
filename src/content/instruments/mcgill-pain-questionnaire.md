## Overview

Melzack's original questionnaire asks people to choose words that describe their pain from three major classes of descriptors (sensory, affective and evaluative), and adds an intensity scale and other items. It yields a pain rating index, the number of words chosen and a present pain intensity score [@melzack1975]. The three classes parallel the sensory, affective and evaluative dimensions of pain proposed by Melzack and Casey [@melzack1968].

The short form (SF-MPQ) keeps 15 descriptors (11 sensory, 4 affective) rated from none to severe, plus the present pain intensity index and a VAS, for situations in which the full MPQ takes too long [@melzack1987]. The SF-MPQ-2 adds descriptors of neuropathic symptoms and switches to a 0 to 10 response format for greater responsiveness, so that one measure covers both neuropathic and non-neuropathic pain [@dworkin2009].

## In research and practice

Pain intensity alone can miss treatment effects on particular qualities of pain, and IMMPACT recommends the SF-MPQ as a secondary outcome for sensory and affective qualities in chronic pain trials [@dworkin2005]. The SF-MPQ-2 was developed for epidemiology, mechanism studies and clinical trials of neuropathic and non-neuropathic pain, with subscales for continuous, intermittent, neuropathic and affective descriptors [@dworkin2009]. It describes pain; it is not a screening tool for neuropathic pain, for which dedicated questionnaires exist.
