## Overview

The FLACC was developed at the University of Michigan to quantify pain behaviours in young children who cannot describe their pain. Its first study observed 89 children aged 2 months to 7 years after surgery and found high agreement between raters, lower scores after analgesics and agreement with other pain ratings [@merkel1997].

## Strengths and limits

It is quick and its 0 to 10 total resembles the familiar numeric scale. A later study in critically ill adults and children found high agreement between nurses, high internal consistency and close correlation with other behavioural scales [@voepellewis2010]. The evidence in each population comes from fairly small samples, and a behaviour score is an observer's judgement, not the person's own rating.
