## Overview

Roland and Morris developed the questionnaire because trials of back pain treatment lacked suitable outcome measures; it measures self-rated disability due to back pain [@roland1983]. The person ticks the statements that apply, and the score is the count. Roland and Fairbank later reviewed the RMDQ together with the Oswestry questionnaire [@roland2000]; the developer's site notes that the copy printed in that review has an error in item 18 and provides the corrected version.

## Strengths and limits

IMMPACT gives the Roland-Morris scale as an example of the disease-specific measures of physical functioning it recommends when one is available [@dworkin2005], and lists it with the ODI as well validated in low back pain [@dworkin2008]. Compared head to head with the ODI, it has better construct validity but weaker test-retest reliability and larger measurement error, with no strong reason to prefer either [@chiarotto2016]. A change of 5 points has been proposed as the minimal important change [@ostelo2008]. It is specific to back pain and is free to use.
