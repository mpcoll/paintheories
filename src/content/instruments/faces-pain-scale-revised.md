## Overview

The FPS-R was adapted from the seven-face Faces Pain Scale of Bieri and colleagues. Hicks and colleagues reduced it to six faces so that scores fit the common 0 to 10 metric, and used psychophysical methods with computer-animated faces to choose faces at equal intervals between least and most pain [@hicks2001]. In their studies most children, including the youngest, used the FPS-R consistently with the other measures, and the authors concluded it is appropriate for acute pain from age 4 or 5 onward and conforms closely to a linear interval scale [@hicks2001].

## In research and practice

A systematic review found that the four extensively tested faces scales are all adequately supported, and saw no grounds to switch from one to another in clinical use [@tomlinson2010]. Children preferred the Wong-Baker scale when given a choice, but its smiling and crying anchor faces confound pain intensity with affect; for research, the FPS-R was recommended [@tomlinson2010]. Evidence is thin for children under 5, for whom simpler measures and instructions are still needed [@tomlinson2010].
