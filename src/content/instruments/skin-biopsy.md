## Overview

Thin nerve fibres end freely in the epidermis, where they can be stained and counted in a small skin sample. The EFNS/PNS guideline recommends a 3 mm biopsy from the distal leg, quantified with agreed counting rules and always compared with age-matched normative values [@lauria2010]; worldwide reference values for the distal leg are available [@lauria2010b]. In about 35,000 biopsies reported by 10 laboratories there were no serious side effects and non-serious side effects in 0.19% [@lauria2010].

## In research and practice

The NeuPSIG guideline recommends skin biopsy in patients with clinical signs of small fibre dysfunction [@haanpaa2011]. It shows a loss of fibres but not the cause of the neuropathy, and a low density is associated with the risk of neuropathic pain but not with how intense the pain is [@lauria2010]. In research it has been used to look for small fibre pathology in conditions without an obvious nerve lesion: in a meta-analysis of fibromyalgia studies, the pooled prevalence of small fibre pathology was 45% (95% CI 32 to 59%) when assessed by skin biopsy [@grayston2019].
