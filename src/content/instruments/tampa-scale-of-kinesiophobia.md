## Overview

The TSK asks how far people agree with statements about movement, injury and pain. It was introduced by Kori, Miller and Todd [@kori1990], and its Dutch version was used by Vlaeyen and colleagues to show that fear of movement/(re)injury relates to catastrophizing and to avoidance of movement in chronic low back pain [@vlaeyen1995]. That work fed directly into the fear-avoidance model [@vlaeyen2000].

## Strengths and limits

Several versions are in use, which makes scores hard to compare: a review identified the TSK-17, TSK-13, TSK-11, TSK-4 and a version for temporomandibular disorders [@dupuis2023]. The TSK-11 drops six items with poor psychometric behaviour and performs about as well as the full scale in chronic low back pain [@woby2005], although its construct validity has been less consistent across studies than that of the longer versions [@dupuis2023].
