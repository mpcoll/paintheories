## Overview

Pain is one of the most frequent and disabling symptoms of cancer. Its prevalence rises with disease stage, from about two in five people after curative treatment to two in three in advanced disease [@vandenbeuken2016]. Because more people now survive cancer for many years, long-term pain from cancer treatment is an increasing problem [@bennett2019].

## How it is classified

Before ICD-11, cancer-related pain had no place in the classification. ICD-11 now distinguishes **chronic cancer pain**, caused by the tumour or metastases, from **chronic post-cancer treatment pain**, caused by surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy or other treatments [@bennett2019; @who2026].

## Mechanisms

Cancer pain is usually mixed. Nociceptive pain from tumour invasion of bone, soft tissue and organs is common. Neuropathic mechanisms contribute in a substantial minority, particularly when treatment damages nerves [@bennett2012].

## Open questions

- Neuropathic components are often missed because clinical assessment methods vary and confirmatory testing is rarely done [@bennett2012].
- How pain mechanisms change from active disease to survivorship, where pain from cancer, its treatment and unrelated causes may coexist [@bennett2019].
