## Overview

Fibromyalgia is defined by chronic widespread pain together with fatigue, unrefreshing sleep and problems with memory and concentration. Clinical examination and routine tests show no tissue damage or inflammation that would account for the pain [@clauw2014]. It is diagnosed far more often in women than in men [@heidari2017].

## How it is classified

In ICD-11, fibromyalgia falls under chronic widespread pain (MG30.01), a subtype of chronic primary pain: pain treated as a condition in its own right rather than a symptom of another disease [@treede2019; @nicholas2019]. In the IASP mechanistic framework it is the reference example of nociplastic pain [@fitzcharles2021].

## Mechanisms

Current accounts emphasise augmented central pain and sensory processing and altered pain modulation, rather than a peripheral source of nociception [@clauw2014; @fitzcharles2021]. Patients often report other bodily symptoms and sensitivities, which fits a nervous-system-wide change in sensory processing.

## Open questions

- How far the nociplastic label explains, rather than describes, the condition is debated [@cohen2022].
- Fibromyalgia overlaps with other chronic primary pain conditions such as irritable bowel syndrome; a shared vulnerability is plausible but its biology is unresolved.
