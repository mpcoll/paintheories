## Overview

Tension-type headache is the most prevalent neurological disorder worldwide. Attacks are typically bilateral, pressing or tightening, mild to moderate in intensity and not made worse by routine physical activity [@ashina2021]. The Global Burden of Disease Study estimated that 1.89 billion people had tension-type headache in 2016; because its disability weight is much lower, it caused far less disability than migraine [@stovner2018]. Diagnosis rests on the headache history and on excluding other causes, using the criteria of the International Classification of Headache Disorders [@ashina2021; @ihs2018].

## How it is classified

ICHD-3 divides tension-type headache by frequency into infrequent episodic, frequent episodic and chronic forms [@ihs2018]. Only the chronic form, with headache on 15 or more days a month, qualifies as chronic pain. In ICD-11 it belongs to chronic primary headache or orofacial pain, a subtype of chronic primary pain, but it is coded with its own neurology code, 8A81.2 [@who2026; @nicholas2019].

## Mechanisms

The biology is unresolved. The leading account holds that peripheral mechanisms, probably in the pericranial muscles and fasciae, generate the pain, while central sensitization contributes to the transformation from episodic to chronic headache [@ashina2021]. People with chronic tension-type headache show increased pericranial tenderness and lower pressure and electrical pain thresholds, including at sites away from the head, which suggests sensitization in the trigeminal nucleus and dorsal horn and at supraspinal levels [@bendtsen2000]. This is why tension-type headache is often cited alongside fibromyalgia as a condition in which nociplastic pain occurs on its own [@fitzcharles2021].

## Open questions

- Which peripheral tissue, if any, provides the nociceptive input that starts the process has not been identified [@bendtsen2000; @ashina2021].
- Despite its prevalence, understanding of tension-type headache has advanced little since the early 2000s, which a recent review attributes to a lack of research attention and funding [@ashina2021].
