## Overview

Irritable bowel syndrome is one of the commonest disorders of gut-brain interaction. Its defining feature is recurrent abdominal pain associated with bowel habit, without a structural or biochemical abnormality that explains it [@lacy2016; @ford2017]. How common it appears depends strongly on the criteria: in a 33-country survey, 4.1% of adults met the stricter Rome IV criteria and 10.1% the earlier Rome III criteria [@sperber2021].

## How it is classified

Gastroenterology classifies irritable bowel syndrome with the Rome criteria [@lacy2016]. ICD-11 gives it a digestive-system code but lists it under **chronic primary visceral pain**, the chronic primary pain subtype for pain in the chest, abdomen or pelvis that is not better explained by another diagnosis [@who2026; @nicholas2019].

## Mechanisms

Proposed mechanisms include altered gut-brain communication and visceral hypersensitivity [@ford2017]. Nociplastic mechanisms have been proposed to contribute, often alongside nociceptive input from the gut [@chu2026].

## Open questions

- Whether visceral hypersensitivity is primarily a peripheral or a central phenomenon, and whether this differs between subgroups.
- Why irritable bowel syndrome so often co-occurs with other chronic primary pain conditions such as fibromyalgia.
