## Overview

The European Society for the Study of Interstitial Cystitis renamed the condition bladder pain syndrome because "interstitial cystitis" had come to mean different things in different centres [@vandemerwe2008]. In a US population survey, 2.7% to 6.5% of adult women met symptom criteria, but few had been diagnosed [@berry2011]. The American Urological Association guideline describes it as a heterogeneous syndrome with subgroups, of which patients with Hunner lesions are the clearest [@clemens2022].

## How it is classified

ICD-11 treats bladder pain without an identifiable bladder disease as chronic primary visceral pain (MG30.00); "chronic primary bladder pain syndrome" is one of its index terms. Interstitial cystitis (GC00.3) is a separate code for an inflammatory bladder disease. Which applies depends on whether bladder inflammation, such as Hunner lesions, is found [@nicholas2019; @aziz2019].

## Mechanisms

Patients with and without Hunner lesions differ in demographics, symptoms, urinary markers and treatment response, so they may be two distinct phenotypes [@lai2020]. In a large cohort of women and men with urological chronic pelvic pain syndromes, pain often spread beyond the pelvis, and widespread pain went with poorer psychosocial health rather than worse bladder symptoms [@lai2017]. In a population sample, interstitial cystitis, vulvodynia, irritable bowel syndrome and fibromyalgia frequently co-occurred [@reed2012], which is consistent with a shared vulnerability to nociplastic pain.

## Open questions

- Whether Hunner-lesion and non-Hunner disease have different pain mechanisms; their mechanistic differences are largely unstudied [@lai2020].
- In non-Hunner disease, whether the bladder is the source of pain or mainly the site where a wider sensitivity shows [@lai2017].
