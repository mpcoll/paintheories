## Overview

Complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS) is a painful condition, usually of one limb, that develops after an injury or occasionally without one [@demos2007; @birklein2018]. Alongside severe pain it involves abnormal sensory, vasomotor, sweating, swelling, motor and trophic signs, and it can be triggered by a minor injury [@marinus2011; @mangnus2023]. In a Dutch population study the incidence was about 26 per 100,000 person-years; women, especially after the menopause, were most often affected, the arm more often than the leg, and a fracture was the commonest trigger (44%) [@demos2007].

## How it is classified

Diagnosis is clinical, based on internationally agreed criteria [@birklein2018]. The Budapest criteria kept the high sensitivity of the earlier IASP criteria while improving specificity [@harden2010]. Type I occurs without, and type II with, an identifiable nerve injury [@marinus2011]. In ICD-11, CRPS is a form of chronic primary pain (MG30.04) [@nicholas2019]; CRPS type II is cross-listed under chronic post traumatic pain but keeps the same code, and the WHO definition stresses that the pain is regional rather than confined to a nerve territory or dermatome [@who2026].

## Mechanisms

CRPS is regarded as multifactorial: aberrant inflammation, vasomotor dysfunction and maladaptive neuroplasticity all contribute, and their relative weight probably varies between patients [@marinus2011; @birklein2018]. Central changes include a smaller representation of the affected hand in the primary somatosensory cortex, which correlated with pain in an early study, although the overall evidence is limited [@maihofner2003; @dipietro2013]. In interviews, patients described disturbed perception of the affected limb, such as parts missing from their mental image of it, and some wished to have it amputated [@lewis2007]. Because nociceptive, neuropathic and nociplastic contributions can coexist and change over time, mechanism-based subgroups have been proposed to guide treatment [@mangnus2023].

## Open questions

- Is CRPS type I a minimal nerve injury (and so neuropathic), a nociplastic condition, or a mixture that changes over time [@oaklander2006; @mangnus2023]?
- No single biomarker has been found; whether a panel of markers can support diagnosis and predict outcome is being studied [@birklein2018].
