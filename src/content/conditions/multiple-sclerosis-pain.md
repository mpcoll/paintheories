## Overview

Pain is common in multiple sclerosis. A meta-analysis put the pooled prevalence at 63%, with headache in 43%, neuropathic extremity pain in 26%, back pain in 20%, Lhermitte's sign in 16%, painful spasms in 15% and trigeminal neuralgia in about 4% of people studied [@foley2013]. A later meta-analysis estimated that about 22% have neuropathic pain [@jungersen2026].

## How it is classified

Because the pains have different mechanisms, they are classified separately. In ICD-11, neuropathic pain from MS lesions is chronic central neuropathic pain (MG30.50), whereas pain mainly due to spasticity is classified as chronic secondary musculoskeletal pain (MG30.32) [@scholz2019; @perrot2019]. A mechanism-based scheme distinguishes nine types of MS-related pain, from paroxysmal neuropathic pains to nociceptive musculoskeletal pain and pain caused by treatment [@truini2012].

## Mechanisms

Ongoing burning pain in the legs and arms is attributed to deafferentation from lesions in the spino-thalamo-cortical pathways, while trigeminal neuralgia and Lhermitte's sign are thought to arise from ectopic impulses generated in demyelinated primary afferents. Painful tonic spasms and spasticity pain are mixed: they follow lesions of central motor pathways but are signalled by muscle nociceptors [@truini2012].

## Open questions

- How pain relates to disease activity, relapses and progression is poorly described, because few studies follow people over the course of the disease [@foley2013].
- Prevalence estimates for neuropathic pain vary widely with diagnostic methods; how best to apply neuropathic pain grading in a disease with widespread central lesions is unsettled [@jungersen2026].
