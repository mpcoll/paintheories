## Overview

Post-traumatic headache is a highly disabling secondary headache and one of the commonest sequelae of mild traumatic brain injury, or concussion [@ashina2019]. A systematic review of people with traumatic brain injury found chronic headache in about 58%, and among civilians chronic pain overall was more common after mild than after moderate or severe injury [@nampiaparampil2008]. Most persistent post-traumatic headaches look like a primary headache: migraine-like and tension-type-like phenotypes are the most common [@ashina2019].

## How it is classified

ICHD-3 attributes a headache to head injury when it begins within 7 days of the injury, and calls it persistent when it lasts more than 3 months [@ihs2018]. ICD-11 has matching neurology codes for acute (8A84.0) and persistent (8A84.1) headache after head injury [@who2026]. The chronic pain chapter has no dedicated code: chronic post traumatic pain is defined around pain at the injured tissue, while chronic secondary headache covers headaches with an identified cause, so persistent post-traumatic headache fits best in the latter's residual category [@who2026; @schug2019; @benoliel2019].

## Mechanisms

Animal and human studies point to several possible mechanisms for the development and persistence of the headache: impaired descending pain modulation, neurometabolic changes, neuroinflammation and activation of the trigeminal sensory system [@ashina2019]. Comparing it with the primary headaches it resembles could reveal shared mechanisms as well as treatment targets specific to post-traumatic headache [@ashina2019].

## Open questions

- No biomarkers yet identify which people will progress from acute to persistent post-traumatic headache [@ashina2019].
- How persistent post-traumatic headache differs biologically from migraine and tension-type headache, which it closely resembles, is not known [@ashina2019].
