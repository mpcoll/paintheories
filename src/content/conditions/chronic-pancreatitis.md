## Overview

Chronic pancreatitis is a fibro-inflammatory syndrome of the pancreas in people with genetic, environmental or other risk factors, such as alcohol and smoking. Ongoing inflammation leads to loss of the gland's digestive and hormonal functions, with recurrent or constant abdominal pain, diabetes and maldigestion [@kleeff2017]. Its incidence and prevalence are rising and there is no curative treatment [@beyer2020], although it remains an uncommon disease [@yadav2011].

## How it is classified

ICD-11 codes the pain as chronic secondary visceral pain, together with chronic pancreatitis itself (DC32). The IASP Task Force notes that in secondary visceral pain the pain is not necessarily matched to the disease process and may persist after the underlying cause has been treated successfully [@aziz2019].

## Mechanisms

Abdominal pain can come from recurrent or chronic inflammation, from local complications and from changes in the nervous system [@drewes2017]. A review groups the mechanisms into peripheral sensitization, pancreatic neuropathy and neuroplastic changes in central pain pathways, with flares accelerating the process [@olesen2017]. Tissue studies show remodelled pancreatic nerves and activated glia whose changes vary with pain [@ceyhan2009]. Consistent with central contributions, drugs that act on the nervous system help a subset of patients [@olesen2017].

## Open questions

- How to tell when pain has become independent of the gland, so that duct drainage or surgery is unlikely to help, before such procedures are done [@olesen2017; @drewes2017].
- Whether pancreatic neuropathy should be classified as neuropathic pain or remain within visceral inflammatory pain.
