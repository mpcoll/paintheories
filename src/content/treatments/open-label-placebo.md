## Overview

Open-label placebos avoid the deception of conventional placebo use. In an early trial in chronic low back pain, 97 adults were randomized to placebo pills, presented in a positive context, added to usual care for 3 weeks, or usual care alone; pain and disability fell more with the placebo pills [@carvalho2016].

## Evidence

Across medical conditions, the first meta-analysis found a significant overall effect of open-label placebos against no treatment and described the research as in its infancy [@vonwernsdorff2021]. An update with 60 trials found a small overall effect that was larger for self-reported than for objective outcomes [@fendel2025]. In chronic musculoskeletal pain, seven trials showed low-certainty benefits on self-reported pain and physical function but, with moderate certainty, no effect on physical function tests; the authors stress that the clinical value is not yet established [@borg2025].

In the trial of pain reprocessing therapy, an open-label saline injection in the back was one of the control arms: mean pain after treatment was 2.84 against 3.13 with usual care [@ashar2022], and at 5 years placebo had no significant effect against usual care on any outcome [@ashar2025].

## Open questions

- Participants know what they receive and rate their own pain, so all trials are at high risk of bias for self-reported outcomes, and effects are smaller on objective outcomes [@borg2025; @fendel2025].
- Most trials last weeks; whether effects persist, and which instructions and settings matter, is not known [@borg2025; @vonwernsdorff2021].
