## Overview

Amitriptyline has been a first-line treatment for neuropathic pain and fibromyalgia for decades, usually at doses below those used for depression [@moore2015; @moore2015b]. Its analgesic action is attributed mainly to strengthening noradrenergic inhibition of the spinal cord [@obata2017].

## Evidence

The 2025 NeuPSIG meta-analysis gives TCAs the lowest NNT among first-line drugs, about 5, with moderate certainty [@soliman2025]. The trials are mostly older, smaller and with lower placebo responses than recent trials of SNRIs or pregabalin, so direct comparisons between classes are unreliable [@soliman2025]. Cochrane reviews that apply stricter standards found no unbiased evidence for amitriptyline in neuropathic pain or fibromyalgia, but no good evidence of a lack of effect either; the concern is overestimation of the effect, and only a minority of people get satisfactory relief [@moore2015; @moore2015b].

## In practice

TCAs cost less than most alternatives [@finnerup2015] and remain first line for neuropathic pain, except in older adults [@soliman2025]. NICE lists amitriptyline among the antidepressants to consider for chronic primary pain, explaining that they may help quality of life, pain, sleep and distress even without depression [@nice2021], but advises against antidepressants for low back pain [@nice2016].
