## Overview

Botulinum toxin type A is best known for relaxing muscles, but it also blocks the release of pain-related transmitters from sensory nerve endings, and its effects last for months after a single injection [@matak2019]. In 2019 chronic migraine was its only approved pain indication, while controlled studies in neuropathic pain were promising [@matak2019].

## Evidence

In neuropathic pain the 2025 NeuPSIG meta-analysis found a large effect, with an NNT below 3, but from only six small trials, mostly in refractory peripheral neuropathic pain [@soliman2025]. In chronic migraine, the Cochrane review estimated about 2 fewer migraine days a month than placebo when small trials were excluded, and high-quality evidence for about 1.9 fewer headache days; evidence in episodic migraine is very uncertain [@herd2018].

## In practice

NeuPSIG recommends botulinum toxin third line for localised peripheral neuropathic pain [@soliman2025]. Its sustained effect after a single application and its safety are practical advantages [@matak2019], while access is restricted [@soliman2025].
