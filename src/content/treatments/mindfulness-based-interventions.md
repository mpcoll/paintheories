## Overview

Mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR) is the best-known format: in the largest trial it was delivered as 8 weekly 2-hour groups of mindfulness meditation and yoga [@cherkin2016]. Trials also test mindfulness-based cognitive therapy and other programmes, often as an add-on to usual care [@hilton2016].

## Evidence

A meta-analysis of 38 trials found low-quality evidence that mindfulness meditation reduces chronic pain slightly compared with all types of control, with high heterogeneity and possible publication bias; the effect was smaller in the better-quality trials. A small improvement in depression was rated high quality and an improvement in mental health-related quality of life moderate quality [@hilton2016]. An earlier meta-analysis that pooled acceptance- and mindfulness-based interventions found effects that were not superior to traditional CBT [@veehof2016].

In chronic low back pain, a trial of 342 adults found that both MBSR and CBT gave more people a clinically meaningful improvement in pain and function at 26 weeks than usual care, with no significant difference between MBSR and CBT; the MBSR effects persisted with little change at 52 weeks [@cherkin2016].

## In practice

The ACP recommends MBSR as a first-choice option for chronic low back pain [@qaseem2017], whereas NICE found too little evidence on mindfulness for chronic primary pain to recommend it either way [@nice2021].
