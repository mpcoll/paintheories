## Overview

Capsaicin acts on TRPV1, the receptor that makes nociceptors respond to heat and to chilli [@caterina1997]. The 8% patch delivers about 100 times more capsaicin than conventional creams in a single application, with effects expected to last about 12 weeks [@derry2017]; NeuPSIG suggests 1 to 4 patches for 30 to 60 minutes, at least 60 days apart [@soliman2025]. Its benefit is attributed to reversible defunctionalisation of nociceptor fibres in the skin rather than to depletion of substance P [@anand2011].

## Evidence

In the NeuPSIG meta-analysis the 8% patch had a small effect (NNT 13) but almost no withdrawals for adverse events; the cream had a lower NNT but very low certainty evidence and a result that could easily become non-significant [@soliman2025]. The Cochrane review found that about 10% more people with postherpetic neuralgia, HIV neuropathy or painful diabetic neuropathy were much improved with the patch than with a low-concentration control, and that those who responded often also gained in sleep, fatigue, depression and quality of life [@derry2017].

## In practice

NeuPSIG recommends topical capsaicin second line for peripheral neuropathic pain confined to an area small enough to treat with patches [@soliman2025]. It is a clear example of a treatment that targets the peripheral nociceptor itself.
