  summary: Gabapentin and pregabalin bind the α2δ-1 subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels, which is upregulated in sensory neurons after nerve injury. They reduce trafficking of α2δ-1 to the central terminals of primary afferents in the dorsal horn and are thought to reduce transmitter release, damping dorsal horn responses; they may also restore deficient descending noradrenergic inhibition. ## Overview

Gabapentin and pregabalin were developed as antiepileptics. Their analgesic target is the α2δ-1 calcium channel subunit, which increases in sensory neurons after nerve injury; this helps explain why they reduce neuropathic hypersensitivity but not normal pain [@patel2016].

## Evidence

Across neuropathic pain, NeuPSIG estimates that about 9 people need to be treated for one more to respond than with placebo [@soliman2025]. Cochrane reviews show the clearest benefit in postherpetic neuralgia and painful diabetic neuropathy, where around 3 or 4 in 10 people on gabapentin achieve at least 50% relief compared with 1 or 2 in 10 on placebo [@wiffen2017]; pregabalin shows efficacy in the same conditions but not in HIV neuropathy [@derry2019]. In a placebo-controlled trial in 209 people with acute or chronic sciatica, pregabalin did not reduce leg pain at 8 or 52 weeks and caused more adverse events [@mathieson2017].

## In practice

Gabapentinoids are first-line options for neuropathic pain [@soliman2025], but NICE advises against starting them for chronic primary pain and against offering them for low back pain or sciatica [@nice2021; @nice2016]. Because of the higher risk of drug-related death when α2δ-ligands are combined with opioids, NeuPSIG asks prescribers to assess the applicable risks systematically [@soliman2025]. Over half of people treated with gabapentin do not get worthwhile relief but may still have adverse events [@wiffen2017].
