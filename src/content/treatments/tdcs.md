## Overview

tDCS delivers a weak current through scalp electrodes and can raise or lower cortical excitability depending on polarity [@nitsche2000]. As with rTMS, the main studied target for pain is the motor cortex, with anodal stimulation over the side opposite the pain [@lefaucheur2016].

## Evidence

In the 2018 Cochrane review of non-invasive brain stimulation, tDCS reduced pain more than sham in the short term; the point estimate met the review's threshold for a minimal clinically important difference (15%), but the lower confidence limit was well below it, the analyses showed small-study bias, and there was no evidence of improvement in disability [@oconnell2018]. Quality of life improved compared with sham (SMD 0.66, 95% CI 0.21 to 1.11; low-quality evidence) [@oconnell2018].

## Open questions

- Whether effects last beyond the short term; the review called for much larger, rigorously designed trials, particularly of longer courses of stimulation [@oconnell2018].
- The 2025 NeuPSIG recommendations for neuropathic pain cover non-invasive neuromodulation, but among these techniques the published abstract names only rTMS in its recommendations [@soliman2025].
