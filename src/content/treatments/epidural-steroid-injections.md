## Overview

Epidural corticosteroid injections are used for lumbosacral radicular pain, commonly called sciatica: a syndrome in which people report pain radiating down the leg [@oliveira2020].

## Evidence

The 2020 Cochrane review (25 trials, 2470 participants) updated a 2012 meta-analysis by the same group [@pinto2012]. It found that injections probably slightly reduce leg pain and disability at short-term follow-up compared with placebo injections, but the differences are small and may not matter to patients or clinicians [@oliveira2020]. In the earlier analysis, effects at 12 months or more were smaller and not statistically significant [@pinto2012].

## In practice

NICE suggests considering epidural local anaesthetic and steroid for acute and severe sciatica, advises against epidural injections for neurogenic claudication in central spinal canal stenosis [@nice2016], and separately advises against spinal injections for managing low back pain [@nice2016]. Benefit, when present, is mainly short-lived [@oliveira2020].
