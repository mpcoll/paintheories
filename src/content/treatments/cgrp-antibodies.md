## Overview

Migraine is now seen as a disorder of sensory processing in which the trigeminal durovascular pathway carries the head pain, and CGRP released from trigeminal neurons is a key signal in that pathway [@goadsby2017; @edvinsson2018]. Four antibodies are available: three bind CGRP itself and erenumab blocks its receptor [@edvinsson2018].

## Evidence

In a network meta-analysis of phase 3 trials in episodic and chronic migraine, every antibody regimen except eptinezumab 30 mg reduced monthly migraine days compared with placebo, and all had higher rates of at least 50% reduction [@haghdoost2023]. The average benefit over placebo is about 1.5 days a month in episodic and about 2 days in chronic migraine; every trial was industry funded but at low risk of bias, and about two-thirds of the improvement in the antibody groups was also seen with placebo, which points to large contextual effects [@forbes2020]. There are no head-to-head trials between the antibodies [@haghdoost2023].

## In practice

The European Headache Federation strongly recommends all four antibodies for preventing episodic and chronic migraine, based on moderate to high quality evidence, and considers them effective and safe in the long term [@sacco2022]. None of the general chronic pain guidelines used on this site covers migraine prevention.
