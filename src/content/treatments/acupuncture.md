## Overview

Acupuncture is widely used for chronic pain but remains controversial, mainly because of how its effects compare with sham needling [@vickers2018].

## Evidence

The Acupuncture Trialists' Collaboration pooled raw data from 20,827 patients in 39 trials of musculoskeletal pain, osteoarthritis, chronic headache and shoulder pain, including only trials with clearly adequate allocation concealment. Acupuncture was better than both sham and no acupuncture for every condition, and about 85% of the effect remained at 1 year. As an illustration, on a 0 to 100 scale starting at 60, follow-up scores might be 43 with no acupuncture, 35 with sham and 30 with acupuncture. Effects were smaller in trials that used penetrating sham needles or gave the control group intensive care, and did not depend clearly on how acupuncture was delivered [@vickers2018].

NICE reached different conclusions in different guidelines. For chronic primary pain it found 27 studies showing short-term (up to 3 months) benefit over usual care or sham, judged that the benefit over sham indicated a specific effect, and recommended a limited, low-cost course on cost-effectiveness grounds [@nice2021]. For osteoarthritis it found a lack of benefit and recommended against [@nice2022], and its 2016 advice not to offer acupuncture for low back pain still stands [@nice2016].

## Open questions

- Blinding is hard: sham procedures can themselves have effects, and the choice of sham changes the estimated effect size [@vickers2018; @nice2021].
- No evidence was found to guide repeat courses of acupuncture [@nice2021].
