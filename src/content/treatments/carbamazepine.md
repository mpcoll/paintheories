## Overview

Trigeminal neuralgia responds to drugs differently from other neuropathic pain, and carbamazepine and oxcarbazepine are its first-choice long-term treatments [@bendtsen2019; @bendtsen2020]. Both block sodium channels in a frequency-dependent way, stabilising hyperexcited membranes and inhibiting repetitive firing [@distefano2018].

## Evidence

Placebo-controlled trials of carbamazepine in trigeminal neuralgia are old and small, but show a very low number needed to treat; oxcarbazepine reduced pain attacks about as well as carbamazepine, with better tolerability and fewer drug interactions [@distefano2018]. The Cochrane review, which applies stricter standards, found only low-tier evidence across trigeminal neuralgia, diabetic neuropathy and post-stroke pain, and concluded that carbamazepine is probably effective in some people with chronic neuropathic pain, with caveats [@wiffen2014].

## In practice

The European Academy of Neurology guideline recommends carbamazepine or oxcarbazepine first, with lamotrigine, gabapentin, botulinum toxin type A, pregabalin, baclofen or phenytoin alone or as add-on, and surgery if pain is not controlled or treatment is poorly tolerated [@bendtsen2019]. Outside trigeminal neuralgia, NeuPSIG rates the evidence as inconclusive [@soliman2025].
