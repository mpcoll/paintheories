## Overview

The lidocaine plaster delivers local anaesthetic to the painful area, and lidocaine belongs to the use-dependent sodium channel blockers proposed for neuropathic pain [@tanelian1991]. Its effect on sensation is limited to small fibres and varies widely between people, which might explain why some patients respond and others do not, a hypothesis that remains to be tested [@krumova2012].

## Evidence

The evidence is weak. NeuPSIG found an NNT of about 15 from three placebo-controlled studies, with very low certainty [@soliman2025]. The Cochrane review found no good-quality trials: studies were small, often cross-over, short or enriched, and all were at high risk of bias, although individual studies suggested benefit and clinical experience supports efficacy in some patients [@derry2014].

## In practice

NeuPSIG suggests lidocaine plaster second line for localised peripheral neuropathic pain, and possibly first line in vulnerable people such as older adults or those taking many medicines [@soliman2025]. NICE advises against starting topical local anaesthetics for chronic primary pain [@nice2021].
