title: How do other people shape pain — reinforcement, support-seeking or communication?
order: 7
summary: Social models make different claims about how others' responses affect pain. Operant theory sees partner responses as reinforcement; communal coping sees catastrophizing as a bid for support; social communication theory focuses on how pain is expressed and decoded; interpersonal fear-avoidance focuses on caregivers' fear. These predict different temporal sequences in everyday interactions.
questions: [variability, disability, function]
predictions:
  - theory: operant-model
    prediction: Solicitous responses that follow pain behaviour should increase subsequent pain behaviour, and pain reports should rise when a solicitous partner is present.
  - theory: communal-coping-model
    prediction: People who catastrophize should express more pain specifically when others are present, and their expression should elicit support and proximity.
  - theory: social-communication-model
    prediction: Observers' estimates of pain should depend on the expression cues available and on observer characteristics, producing systematic and predictable under-estimation.
  - theory: interpersonal-fear-avoidance-model
    prediction: Caregiver fear and catastrophizing should increase protective behaviour, which should precede increases in the person's avoidance and disability.
test:
  design: Dyadic ecological momentary assessment in couples or parent–child dyads with time-lagged multilevel models, combined with laboratory sessions that manipulate observer presence and the type of observer response.
  decisive: The direction and timing of lagged effects — whether partner responses predict later pain behaviour (operant), catastrophizing predicts later support-seeking (communal coping), or caregiver fear predicts later avoidance (interpersonal fear-avoidance).
references: [flor1987, sullivan2001, thorn2003, vlaeyen2009, hadjistavropoulos2011]

## Current evidence

Perceived spouse reinforcement predicted pain reports and activity levels [@flor1987]. The communal coping model was proposed to explain catastrophizing as an interpersonal strategy [@sullivan2001; @thorn2003], but in an experiment threat and social presence affected pain expression independently of catastrophizing [@vlaeyen2009]. A biopsychosocial model of pain communication links expression and decoding [@hadjistavropoulos2011]. Most evidence is cross-sectional, so the temporal sequences these models predict have rarely been tested.
