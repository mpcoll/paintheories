---
title: Do psychological treatments work through the mechanisms their theories propose?
order: 6
summary: Each psychological model implies a treatment and a mechanism of change — fear reduction, changed appraisals, psychological flexibility, altered contingencies or reconceptualised pain. Effects are small on average and similar across approaches, which raises the question of whether treatments act through their specific mechanisms or through shared ones.
questions: [disability, chronicity]
predictions:
  - theory: fear-avoidance-model
    prediction: Exposure should outperform graded activity specifically in high-fear patients, and reductions in fear should precede reductions in disability.
  - theory: cognitive-behavioural-model
    prediction: Changes in appraisals and catastrophizing should precede and mediate improvements in pain and disability.
  - theory: psychological-flexibility-model
    prediction: Increases in acceptance and flexibility should mediate the effects of ACT more than those of other treatments.
  - theory: operant-model
    prediction: Changing contingencies (e.g. partner responses, activity quotas) should reduce pain behaviour and disability even without changes in beliefs.
  - theory: protective-output-account
    prediction: Education should reduce threat appraisals, and that reduction should mediate changes in pain.
test:
  design: A multi-arm dismantling trial comparing exposure, cognitive therapy, ACT, operant treatment and pain education against an attention-matched control, with every proposed mediator measured session by session to test temporal precedence and treatment-specific mediation.
  decisive: Mediators that change specifically in their own arm and precede outcome change would support specific mechanisms. The same mediators changing in all active arms, or outcomes changing before mediators, would point to shared (common) factors.
references: [williams2020, leeuw2008, vlaeyen2001, vowles2014, traeger2019, watson2019]
review: draft
updated: 2026-11-26

## Current evidence

Across trials, psychological therapies have small or very small benefits compared with active controls, and behavioural therapy alone shows no clear benefit [@williams2020]. Exposure in vivo reduced fear and catastrophizing more than graded activity but was not clearly superior for disability [@leeuw2008], despite strong effects in single-case designs [@vlaeyen2001]. Process models of ACT fit observational data [@vowles2014]. Intensive pain education did not reduce pain in acute low back pain [@traeger2019], and the effects of pain neuroscience education are mixed [@watson2019].
