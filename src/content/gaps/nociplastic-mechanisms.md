title: What maintains nociplastic pain — central amplification or peripheral drivers?
order: 4
summary: Nociplastic pain is defined by altered nociception without clear tissue or nerve damage, and is often attributed to central sensitization. But small fibre pathology, autoantibodies and ongoing peripheral input have been found in subgroups, raising the question of whether the "central" state is self-sustaining or maintained from the periphery.
questions: [chronicity, variability]
predictions:
  - theory: central-sensitization
    prediction: Once established, widespread hypersensitivity should persist when peripheral input is blocked, because the amplification is a central state.
  - theory: neuroimmune-theory
    prediction: Immune factors such as patient IgG or glial activation should predict symptom severity and transfer hypersensitivity, and immune-targeted treatment should help the affected subgroup.
  - theory: endogenous-modulation-model
    prediction: Impaired descending inhibition (e.g. reduced conditioned pain modulation) should predict severity and the response to drugs that enhance inhibition.
  - theory: nociplastic-pain
    prediction: The construct is mechanism-agnostic, so it predicts heterogeneity — distinct subgroups with different dominant mechanisms, rather than a single shared one.
test:
  design: In fibromyalgia and related conditions, stratify patients by small fibre pathology and immune markers, then test blinded peripheral blockade versus sham with remote quantitative sensory testing, conditioned pain modulation, and independent, blinded replication of IgG-transfer experiments.
  decisive: If peripheral blockade normalises remote hypersensitivity, central sensitization is maintained from the periphery rather than self-sustaining. Subgroup-specific effects would support a heterogeneous, mechanism-based classification.
references: [kosek2016, kosek2021, staud2001, staud2009, gracely1992, uceyler2013, oaklander2013, goebel2021]

## Current evidence

Patients with fibromyalgia show enhanced temporal summation [@staud2001], consistent with central amplification. Yet tender-point lidocaine reduced remote heat hyperalgesia [@staud2009], and in CRPS a peripheral block abolished allodynia until it wore off [@gracely1992], suggesting that central changes are maintained by peripheral input. Small fibre pathology is present in a large subgroup of people with fibromyalgia [@uceyler2013; @oaklander2013], and patient IgG transferred hypersensitivity to mice [@goebel2021]. The nociplastic descriptor was introduced to avoid committing to a mechanism [@kosek2016; @kosek2021].
