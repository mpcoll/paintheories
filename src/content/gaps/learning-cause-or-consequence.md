title: Are learning differences a cause or a consequence of chronic pain?
order: 8
summary: Learning theories propose that chronic pain is maintained by maladaptive learning — over-generalised fear, impaired safety learning, or altered reward and relief learning. People with chronic pain do show learning differences, but almost all studies are cross-sectional, so these differences may be consequences of living with pain rather than causes.
questions: [chronicity]
predictions:
  - theory: imprecision-hypothesis
    prediction: Broad generalisation and impaired safety learning should be present before pain becomes chronic, and should predict who develops it.
  - theory: classical-conditioning-model
    prediction: Differences in acquisition and extinction should predict persistence, and extinction-based treatment should normalise them along with pain.
  - theory: corticolimbic-learning-theory
    prediction: Learning-related corticostriatal properties should predispose people to chronic pain and predict which learning differences appear after onset.
  - theory: reinforcement-learning-account
    prediction: Computational parameters (learning rates, aversive and relief prediction errors) should differ prospectively in people who go on to develop chronic pain, and should relate to specific symptoms.
test:
  design: Prospective computational phenotyping of conditioning, generalisation, extinction and relief learning in people with acute pain, with repeated testing during recovery or chronification and neuroimaging of corticostriatal circuits.
  decisive: Learning differences present at onset that predict persistence would support a causal role; differences that emerge only after chronification would point to consequences of pain.

## Current evidence

People with chronic pain show reduced differential learning and flatter generalisation gradients [@meulders2014; @meulders2015; @harvie2020], but results are heterogeneous, including for extinction [@meulders2017], and a systematic review concluded that a causal role remains unverified [@harvie2017]. Corticostriatal connectivity predicts chronification [@baliki2012], and learning-based accounts link these lines of work [@vlaeyen2015; @seymour2019].
