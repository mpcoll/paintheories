## Overview

Woolf showed in rats that the increased excitability of the flexion reflex after peripheral injury arises in part from changes in the spinal cord, so that post-injury hypersensitivity has a central as well as a peripheral component [@woolf1983]. Central sensitization is defined as an increased responsiveness of nociceptive neurons in the central nervous system to their normal or sub-threshold input. It depends on activity-dependent synaptic plasticity, including NMDA-receptor-mediated mechanisms, and is amplified by neuroimmune signalling; extensive animal work has characterised its cellular and molecular mechanisms [@woolf2000; @latremoliere2009; @ji2018].

## Critiques

- In humans, central sensitization is inferred indirectly (e.g. from secondary hyperalgesia, allodynia or enhanced temporal summation in quantitative sensory testing). These measures are not specific to spinal mechanisms; brain-level processes such as attention and expectation can produce similar findings.
- The claim that sensitization becomes independent of peripheral input is hard to test, and findings that blocking peripheral input reverses hypersensitivity blur the line between central and peripheral explanations.
- The term is used loosely in clinical practice, sometimes as an explanation for any unexplained pain. This partly motivated the introduction of "nociplastic pain" as a descriptor that makes no specific mechanistic claim [@kosek2016].

## Legacy

Central sensitization is a central concept in the shift from seeing chronic pain as a symptom of tissue damage to seeing it as a state of the nervous system. It has been proposed as a contributor to a wide range of chronic pain conditions, including fibromyalgia, osteoarthritis, temporomandibular disorders and headache [@woolf2011].

## Teaching notes

- Distinguish the *mechanism* (well established in animals) from the *clinical construct* (applied to patients by inference).
- Compare with peripheral sensitization, which involves increased responsiveness of nociceptors themselves.
