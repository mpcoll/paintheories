## Overview

Kosek and colleagues argued that the two established mechanistic descriptors, nociceptive and neuropathic, left a large group of patients unclassified: people with chronic pain and hypersensitivity but without evidence of tissue damage sufficient to explain it or of somatosensory nerve lesion [@kosek2016]. The IASP adopted "nociplastic pain" in 2017, defining it as pain arising from altered nociception despite no clear evidence of actual or threatened tissue damage causing activation of peripheral nociceptors, or of disease or lesion of the somatosensory system. The descriptor is deliberately neutral about mechanism: "plastic" refers to altered function of nociceptive pathways, not to a specific cellular process.

Fitzcharles and colleagues described nociplastic pain as the main mechanism in fibromyalgia, irritable bowel syndrome, chronic primary low back pain and related conditions, noting that it often co-occurs with nociceptive and neuropathic pain [@fitzcharles2021]. Kosek and colleagues then proposed clinical criteria and a grading system for nociplastic pain affecting the musculoskeletal system. Pain must have lasted more than three months, be regional, multifocal or widespread, and not be entirely explained by nociceptive or neuropathic mechanisms. It must also show clinical signs of hypersensitivity, with history of hypersensitivity and comorbidities (sleep, fatigue, cognition) supporting a grade of "probable" [@kosek2021].

## Historical context

The concept grew out of research on fibromyalgia and "central sensitivity" syndromes [@clauw2014] and from dissatisfaction with "central sensitization" as a clinical label. It also complements the IASP's revision of the definition of pain [@raja2020] and the ICD-11 category of chronic primary pain. Kosek and colleagues weighed several candidate terms before proposing "nociplastic" [@kosek2016].

## Critiques

- The category is defined partly by exclusion (no adequate nociceptive or neuropathic explanation), so it may expand or shrink with diagnostic technology. Findings of small fibre pathology and of autoantibody-mediated sensitization in fibromyalgia illustrate this.
- The proposed clinical criteria have been criticised as flawed [@cohen2022]. More generally, a mechanistic label defined by clinical features risks reifying a mechanism that has not been identified.
- Psychophysical signs of hypersensitivity are not specific to nociplastic pain and are common in osteoarthritis and nerve injury [@schmidt2024].
- Proponents themselves stress that it is a mechanistic *descriptor*, not a diagnosis, and that criteria need validation [@kosek2024].

## Legacy

Nociplastic pain is now standard IASP terminology and is widely used in research and clinical phenotyping, for example to identify patients unlikely to benefit from surgery or opioids. Its validity as a distinct category, and the performance of its criteria, are active research questions.

## Teaching notes

- "Nociplastic" is a descriptor of *how* pain arises, not a diagnosis and not a synonym for "psychological".
- Contrast with central sensitization: a specific neurophysiological mechanism versus a deliberately mechanism-agnostic clinical category.
- Discussion prompt: what would count as evidence that nociplastic pain is a natural kind rather than a residual category?
