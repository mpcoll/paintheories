## Overview

The social communication model treats pain as an event between people as well as within one person. Hadjistavropoulos and Craig first set out a communications model to explain how self-report and observational pain measures relate [@hadjistavropoulos2002]. The person's experience, shaped by intrapersonal and contextual factors, is *encoded* in expressive behaviour. Observers then *decode* it and decide how to *act*. Self-report is mainly under control of higher mental processes and can be shaped deliberately. Facial and other non-verbal expression is more automatic. It is less open to purposeful distortion, but observers may find it harder to decode.

Craig developed this into the social communication model [@craig2009]. Each stage, from experience through expression and decoding to caregiver action, has biological, psychological and social determinants. For example, the sufferer's developmental stage and learning history affect expression, while the observer's training, beliefs, empathy and biases affect decoding. A later multi-author formulation integrated perspectives from clinical, developmental, evolutionary and social psychology, communication studies and behavioural neuroscience [@hadjistavropoulos2011].

## Historical context

The model grew out of decades of work on facial expression of pain and on assessing pain in people who cannot reliably self-report, such as infants, people with intellectual disabilities and people with dementia [@hadjistavropoulos2002]. It reacted against two tendencies: treating self-report as the only valid index of pain, and the operant tendency to treat pain behaviour as reinforced display open to suspicion [@williams2002]. Evolutionary accounts of facial expression of pain helped frame expression as a signal that has evolved to elicit help [@williams2002].

## Critiques

- The model is a broad organising framework with many determinants at each stage. It generates research questions more than specific, falsifiable predictions.
- The automatic/controlled distinction between non-verbal and verbal expression is a matter of degree. Facial expression can also be suppressed or exaggerated, so the two channels are not cleanly separable.
- Social context effects on pain are inconsistent across studies, and the model does not say in advance which observer or context will increase and which will decrease pain or its expression [@krahe2013].

## Legacy

The social communication model is the main framework for research on pain expression, observer judgement and caregiver behaviour. It underpins observational pain assessment tools for non-verbal populations and work on pain underestimation and bias by health professionals. It also anchors the interpersonal pain literature, including dyadic and family models such as the communal coping and interpersonal fear-avoidance models.

## Teaching notes

- Draw the experience → encoding → decoding → action chain and place a known finding (e.g. underestimation, racial bias, social threat) at the stage where it acts.
- Discuss why moderate agreement between self-report and pain behaviour can be informative rather than measurement error.
- Contrast the operant and communication readings of the same pain behaviour.
