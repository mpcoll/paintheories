## Overview

Melzack and Casey extended gate control into the brain by proposing that pain has three dimensions supported by different systems [@melzack1968]. The distinction between how intense pain feels and how unpleasant it is became standard in pain measurement (e.g. separate intensity and unpleasantness ratings) [@melzack2012].

## Evidence for

- Intensity and unpleasantness can be modulated separately by hypnotic suggestion and other manipulations.
- Neuroimaging studies associate somatosensory cortices more with intensity and anterior cingulate and insular cortices more with affect, although the mapping is not clean.

## Evidence against and critiques

- The proposed anatomical division (lateral sensory vs medial affective) is too sharp; most regions contribute to several dimensions.

## Legacy

The multidimensional view is now built into the IASP definition of pain as "an unpleasant sensory and emotional experience" [@raja2020] and into most pain questionnaires.
