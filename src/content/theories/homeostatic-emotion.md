## Overview

Craig proposed that pain belongs with the feelings that report the state of the body, such as temperature, itch, muscle ache, hunger and thirst, rather than with exteroceptive touch [@craig2002]. These feelings are carried by small-diameter afferents and lamina I spinal neurons, which project to brainstem homeostatic centres and, in primates, through a dedicated thalamic relay to the dorsal posterior insula. Craig treats this region as primary interoceptive cortex. Because each such feeling has a sensory aspect and a motivational aspect that drives a homeostatic response, he called pain a "homeostatic emotion" [@craig2003].

In this view, the affective character of pain is not added to a sensation by higher cortical processing. It is intrinsic to the interoceptive channel: the insula provides the feeling and the anterior cingulate the motivation. Interoceptive signals are re-represented in the right anterior insula, which Craig later proposed as a substrate of subjective awareness [@craig2009b].

## Historical context

The theory develops Craig's long-standing defence of labeled lines in central pain processing against convergence (wide-dynamic-range) accounts [@craig2003b]. It drew on his identification of modality-selective lamina I neurons and of a thalamic relay (VMpo) and insular targets [@craig1994; @craig2000]. It also offered an explanation of central post-stroke pain and the thermal grill illusion as loss of normal inhibition of pain by cool signals [@craig1996].

## Critiques

- The anatomy is disputed. Other groups argued that lamina I projections in primates are widespread and that VMpo is not a distinct nucleus or a specific pain relay [@willis2002; @graziano2004].
- Lesion cases show that pain, including its unpleasantness, can persist after extensive insular, cingulate and amygdala damage, so these regions may modulate rather than generate pain [@starr2009; @feinstein2015].
- Classing pain as interoceptive is contested. Much pain is about the external world, for example localising a noxious object, and nociception from skin has exteroceptive as well as interoceptive features.
- The broader claim that the anterior insula underlies awareness goes well beyond the pain evidence.

## Legacy

The homeostatic emotion view has been influential in linking pain to interoception, emotion and autonomic regulation, and in placing the posterior insula at the centre of nociceptive cortical processing. It informs interoceptive and predictive accounts of pain and emotion, though its specific anatomical claims remain contested.

## Teaching notes

- Compare with the three-dimensional model: both treat affect as part of pain, but Craig locates it in a dedicated interoceptive pathway rather than in parallel cortical systems.
- Use the lesion cases to discuss what "necessary for pain" means in network accounts.
- Discussion prompt: is pain more like hunger (a bodily need) or like vision (a sense of the world)?
