## Overview

Moseley, Gallace and Spence introduced the "cortical body matrix" to bring together findings from bodily illusions in healthy people and from clinical disorders of body perception [@moseley2012]. The body matrix is a multisensory representation of the body and of the space immediately around it, held in a body-centred frame of reference. It integrates touch, proprioception and vision, and is coupled to systems that regulate and protect the body, including thermoregulation. Ownership, perceived location and physiological regulation of a body part are therefore thought to depend on one another.

Applied to pain, the model proposes that in conditions such as complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS) the body matrix is disrupted. Perceptual deficits, altered body perception, autonomic signs and pain would then share a common cortical source. A key prediction is that some CRPS features depend on the *space* the affected limb occupies relative to the body midline, not only on the limb's anatomy [@moseley2009; @moseley2012b].

## Historical context

The model grew out of neuromatrix ideas and of rubber-hand and mirror-illusion research in the 2000s. It also drew on reports that people with CRPS and chronic back pain perceive the painful body part as distorted and show impaired tactile processing [@lewis2007; @moseley2008c]. It extended earlier cortical accounts of chronic pain, which focused on somatotopic maps, by adding peripersonal space and homeostatic regulation.

## Critiques

- Much of the clinical evidence comes from small samples studied by the proposing group. The CRPS spatial findings would benefit from independent, preregistered replication.
- The illusion-induced cooling result in healthy people, an important link between ownership and physiological regulation, has not replicated reliably and may depend on details of the stimulation procedure [@rohde2013; @dehaan2017].
- The body matrix is defined at a functional level and is not tied to a specific neural circuit. It is therefore hard to falsify and overlaps with other constructs (body schema, body image, peripersonal space).
- Causal direction is unclear: disrupted body representation might follow from pain, disuse or attention rather than cause them.

## Legacy

The concept is influential in rehabilitation research and has informed interventions that target body perception, such as visual manipulation of the limb, mirror therapy and graded motor imagery. As a mechanistic account of CRPS it remains a hypothesis supported mainly by small experimental studies.

## Teaching notes

- Contrast a somatotopic map (S1 "homunculus") with a body-centred spatial representation: the crossed-hands experiments are designed to tell these apart.
- Discuss why a failed replication in healthy volunteers (rubber hand cooling) does or does not bear on the clinical findings in CRPS.
- Link to the protective-output view of pain, which shares proponents and many assumptions.
